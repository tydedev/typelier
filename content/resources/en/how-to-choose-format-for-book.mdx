## What's a Book Format?

When choosing how a book should look and feel, one of the first decisions an author has to make is its **format**.

In modern publishing, *book format* generally refers to the physical dimensions of the finished book — also called its **trim size**. The trim size determines the width and height of the pages after the book has been printed and trimmed.

It may seem like a simple technical specification, but the format has a direct impact on the reading experience. A smaller book can feel compact and portable, while a larger format provides more space for longer lines, illustrations, tables, or other visual elements.

### From the codex to the modern book

The physical structure of the modern book has a long history.

The **codex**, which gradually replaced the scroll in late antiquity and became the dominant form of the book during the Middle Ages, was made from individual sheets or folded gatherings assembled and bound along one edge. The way these sheets were folded and assembled contributed to the different formats used in book production.

With the development of printing, terms such as *folio*, *quarto*, and *octavo* became common ways of describing how a printer's sheet was folded to produce the leaves of a book.

Today, however, authors working with print-on-demand and self-publishing generally encounter a much simpler system: **the dimensions of the finished book**.

This is what is usually meant by *trim size*.

### The most common book formats

There is no single standard size that works for every type of book. Publishers and print-on-demand platforms offer a range of trim sizes, and the most appropriate choice depends on the genre, intended audience, amount of text, and type of content.

Some commonly encountered US book sizes include:

- **Mass Market Paperback — 4.25 × 6.87 in (108 × 175 mm)**
  A compact format traditionally associated with inexpensive, highly portable editions of novels.

- **Trade Paperback — 5.5 × 8.5 in (140 × 216 mm)**
  A versatile format commonly used for fiction and nonfiction. Its relatively compact proportions make it comfortable for novels while still providing enough space for longer text.

- **6 × 9 in (152 × 229 mm)**
  One of the most widely used formats in self-publishing, particularly for novels, nonfiction, memoirs, and other text-heavy books.

- **Hardcover — commonly around 6 × 9 in (152 × 229 mm)**
  Hardcover books are available in many different sizes, but dimensions around 6 × 9 inches are common for novels and nonfiction. Unlike a paperback, however, the hardcover format also includes the dimensions and construction of the case and cover boards.

These are only examples. Picture books, poetry collections, art books, workbooks, cookbooks, and illustrated nonfiction often require substantially different proportions.

### Choosing a format

The best format is not necessarily the largest or the most common one. It should support the **content and reading experience** of the book.

Before choosing a trim size, consider:

1. **Genre:** readers have different expectations for different types of books.
2. **Amount of text:** the same manuscript can produce a very different page count depending on the format.
3. **Typography:** page dimensions affect line length, type size, margins, and overall readability.
4. **Visual content:** illustrations, photographs, tables, and diagrams may require additional page width.
5. **Production cost:** a larger trim size can affect the cost of printing, especially for books with many pages.
6. **Portability:** a smaller book may be more comfortable to carry and hold.

The format is therefore not just a number to enter into a publishing platform. It is one of the first design decisions that shapes the physical character of the book.

Once you have chosen a format, the next step is to determine **how the page itself should be structured**: margins, text area, line length, typography, and the relationship between all of these elements.
